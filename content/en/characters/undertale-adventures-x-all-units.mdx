## How Summoning, Souls, and Upgrades Shape Your Unit Collection

Before you can talk about specific units, you need to understand the economy that produces them. Undertale Adventures X runs on a small set of resources, and every one of them eventually converts into a stronger squad.

Soul Fragments are the raw material. You collect them from chapters, events, and promotional rewards, then combine them into Souls. Souls are the currency you spend to summon new units. Trait Rerolls live in a separate lane entirely — they don't get you new characters, they make your existing ones better, and you spend them in the upgrades area to buff your team's stats.

| Resource | Where it comes from | What it's used for |
|---|---|---|
| Soul Fragments | Chapter clears, events, promo codes | Combined into Souls |
| Souls | Crafted from Soul Fragments | Summoning new units |
| Trait Rerolls | Codes, events, upgrade rewards | Rerolling a unit's trait in the upgrades area |
| Gaster Hands | Rare promo and event rewards | A premium item — community reports suggest saving it for units you know you'll keep |

The takeaway: don't burn rerolls the moment you get them. Rerolls are a late-game multiplier, and spending them on a unit you'll replace next week is one of the most common mistakes new players make.

## Undertale Adventures X All Units by Role: A Practical Breakdown

Rather than memorizing a tier list that changes with every balance patch, learn the roles. Every unit in Undertale Adventures X fits into one of five jobs, and a team that's missing a job will fail stages that a "weaker" but more balanced team clears easily.

### Damage Units

Damage splits into two flavors. Single-target units delete bosses and armored enemies, but they struggle when twenty small enemies walk past them. Area-of-effect units handle swarms and long lanes, but they tickle bosses. You want at least one of each on almost every stage.

### Support and Buffer Units

Support units don't show up on the damage chart, which is exactly why players underrate them. A buffer that increases the damage or attack speed of nearby units effectively multiplies your carries. On longer chapters, one good support can be worth more than a third damage dealer.

### Control and Debuff Units

Control units slow, stun, or weaken enemies. They're the safety net for stages where enemies move fast or spawn in tight clusters. If you keep losing with a full-health base and a wave that slips through, you don't need more damage — you need control.

### Economy and Utility Units

Economy units generate extra income or resources during a run. They're rarely the star of a stage, but on farming runs and long chapters they pay for themselves several times over. Build them into your lineup when you're grinding, and swap them out for raw power when you're pushing new content.

| Role | What it does | Prioritize it when |
|---|---|---|
| Single-target DPS | Melts bosses and tanky enemies | The chapter ends with a boss |
| AoE damage | Clears swarms and long lanes | Early waves keep leaking |
| Support / buffer | Boosts nearby units' damage or speed | Stages run long and stall out |
| Control / debuff | Slows, stuns, or weakens enemies | Fast enemies slip past your defense |
| Economy / utility | Generates extra resources mid-run | You're farming, not pushing |

## Traits and Rerolls: Turning Good Units Into Great Ones

A unit's trait can matter as much as its base stats. Two copies of the same character with different traits can perform completely differently, which is why experienced players treat rerolls as a currency worth hoarding.

The rule of thumb: reroll your carries, never your placeholders. If a unit is going to sit in your lineup for the next twenty chapters, a strong trait multiplies every level and upgrade you invest afterward. If you're going to replace it in a week, a reroll is wasted.

| Situation | Recommended action |
|---|---|
| Main carry with a mediocre trait | Reroll — this is the highest-value use of your stock |
| Placeholder unit you plan to replace | Skip and save the rerolls |
| Unit already has a strong trait for its role | Skip; rerolling can make it worse |
| Large reroll stockpile from codes or events | Reroll your top two or three units only |
| Brand-new unit you haven't tested | Test it first, then decide |

| Progression stage | Where your resources should go |
|---|---|
| Early game | Cheap AoE units plus one dependable damage dealer |
| Mid game | Levels on your two best units before anything else |
| Late game | Trait rerolls on your carries |
| End game | Filling role gaps so no stage hard-counters you |

Community reports consistently point to the same pattern: players who level two units deeply progress faster than players who spread upgrades across eight.

## Team Building and Chapter Planning

A good Undertale Adventures X team isn't five copies of your strongest unit. It's a lineup where each slot has a job, and the jobs cover each other's weaknesses. Use this template as a starting point and adjust per chapter.

| Slot | Role | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AoE damage | Anchors the early waves and prevents leaks |
| 2 | Single-target DPS | Handles the boss or the tanky stragglers |
| 3 | Support | Multiplies the output of slots 1 and 2 |
| 4 | Control | Slows anything that gets past your damage |
| 5 | Flex | Fills whatever the specific chapter punishes |

When a stage beats you, don't immediately blame your units. Check three things first: did you place your damage where the path is longest, did you upgrade your carry before spreading upgrades around, and did you have a control unit for the fast enemies? Most losses come from placement and priority, not from a weak roster.

Keep a written note of which unit carried each chapter. After ten or so stages, patterns emerge, and you'll know exactly which role your collection is missing.

There's no permanent answer, because balance changes and new chapters shift what's strong. Instead of chasing a fixed tier list, aim for coverage: one AoE damage unit, one single-target carry, one support, and one control unit. A balanced lineup clears more content than a pile of top-tier units that all do the same thing.

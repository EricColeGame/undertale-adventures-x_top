## The Core Progression Loop, Step by Step

Understanding the loop is the difference between grinding and progressing. In Undertale Adventures X, the loop is short but easy to break if you skip a step.

1. **Clear chapters.** Every clear feeds Soul Fragments into your account and moves your battle pass progress forward.
2. **Convert Fragments into Souls.** Soul Fragments combine into Souls, which are the currency required to summon units.
3. **Summon or upgrade.** Spend Souls on new units, or invest in the roster you already have.
4. **Reroll traits.** Head to the upgrades area and spend rerolls to buff your team's stats.
5. **Re-test the stage you were stuck on.** Better stats usually mean the difference between a wipe and a clean clear.

| Loop Stage | Resource In | Resource Out | Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter clears | Time, team strength | Soul Fragments, pass XP | Weak roster |
| Fragment conversion | Soul Fragments | Souls | Low clear speed |
| Summoning | Souls | New units | RNG |
| Rerolling | Trait Rerolls | Better stats | Reroll supply |
| Push stages | Better stats | More Fragments | Stage difficulty spike |

Notice where the loop stalls: reroll supply. That is exactly what the battle pass is designed to solve, which is why ignoring it slows everything downstream.

## Tower Roles and Placement Mechanics

Tower defense is a placement game before it is a power game. A maxed tower in the wrong spot loses to an average tower in the right one. Most units in Undertale Adventures X fall into a handful of recognizable archetypes.

| Archetype | Job | Best Placement | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-target DPS | Burn down bosses and tanky enemies | Long straightaways, near the end of the path | Placing at the first corner where range is wasted |
| Splash / AoE | Clear grouped enemies | Corners and switchbacks where enemies bunch up | Over-investing before groups actually form |
| Support / buff | Multiply nearby damage | Center of a cluster of damage towers | Isolating it where it buffs only one unit |
| Control / slow | Buy time for everything else | Early path segments | Placing it late, after enemies already passed |
| Economy | Generate long-term value | Safe back rows | Building too many and failing the early waves |

A balanced squad usually runs two damage archetypes, one support, and one control piece. Community reports consistently suggest that stacking pure damage works early and collapses hard once wave counts spike.

## Building a Squad Around Undertale Adventures X Battlepass Towers

Once you understand roles, synergy becomes the real skill. The strongest squads are not the ones with the rarest units; they are the ones where every tower makes the others better.

| Pairing | Why It Works | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|
| Control + AoE | Slowed enemies stay inside splash zones longer | Control tower dying first to leaks |
| Support + single-target | Buffed DPS shreds bosses that would otherwise stall | Support placed out of range |
| Economy + late-game DPS | Early cash converts into a stronger final board | Greeding economy and losing early waves |
| Double DPS + control | Reliable damage with no hard counters | No support means lower ceiling |

### Rerolls Are the Real Power Spike

A unit with mediocre traits can outperform a rarer unit with bad ones. That is why rerolls are worth hoarding between sessions and spending in batches rather than one at a time. Player experience suggests that saving rerolls for the units you actually field in your main lineup produces far better results than scattering them across the whole roster.

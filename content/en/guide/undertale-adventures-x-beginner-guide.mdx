## What Is Undertale Adventures X?

Undertale Adventures X is a Roblox tower defense game from Side Productions that borrows the Underground setting and cast of the original Undertale, then rebuilds them as deployable units. You place characters along a lane, they attack incoming waves automatically, and you spend in-match currency to upgrade them before enemies reach your base. Between runs, you collect permanent resources, roll traits, and summon new units to widen your roster.

If you've played any Roblox tower defense before, the fundamentals will feel familiar. The differences that matter most are the trait system, which lets you reroll modifiers on individual units, and how heavily unit roles shape whether a stage is winnable at all.

| Genre | Lane-based tower defense with wave clearing and boss stages |
| Developer | Side Productions, per the community wiki |

Because the game updates often, treat any tier list — including the ones on fan wikis — as a snapshot rather than a permanent verdict. A unit that sits in the middle of the pack today can jump after a single balance patch.

## The Core Early-Game Loop, Step by Step

Most new players lose stages for the same reason: they spread resources too thin. The fix is a strict order of operations that you repeat on every stage until it becomes automatic.

1. **Deploy for coverage, not for raw numbers.** Place your first units where they overlap the main enemy path for the longest stretch. Two well-placed units covering the same corner beat four units scattered across the map.
2. **Upgrade your best damage dealer before adding new units.** Early in-match currency is limited. Feeding it into the unit that already does the most work compounds faster than buying a second mediocre tower.
3. **Farm stages you can clear comfortably.** Repeatable clears fund your next round of upgrades. A fast, safe clear is worth more than a risky push that fails at wave 20.
4. **Push one stage at a time.** When a stage fails, identify why — damage, coverage, or survival — and fix that specific gap instead of rebuilding your entire team.
5. **Roll traits only after your team is stable.** Traits amplify what you already have. Rolling them on units you'll replace next week wastes the materials.

| Step | Goal | Common Beginner Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Deploy | Full path coverage | Buying units instead of placing the ones you own |
| 2. Upgrade | Concentrated damage | Spreading currency across every unit equally |
| 3. Farm | Reliable income | Farming the hardest clearable stage instead of the fastest |
| 4. Push | Unlock new content | Rebuilding the team after every single loss |
| 5. Traits | Amplify core units | Rerolling throwaway starter units |

The last point deserves emphasis. Traits are the biggest power multiplier in the game, but they're also the easiest resource to waste. If a unit isn't in your main team, it probably doesn't deserve a reroll yet.

## Unit Roles: Who Does What in Undertale Adventures X

Every unit in the game fits roughly one of five roles. Knowing the role tells you where to place the unit and whether to upgrade it.

| Role | Job on the Team | Upgrade Priority | When You Need It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Damage | Single-target output against tough enemies | High — first investment | Almost every stage and boss |
| Area coverage | Splash, piercing, or multi-target attacks | Medium-high | Dense waves and event stages |
| Utility | Buffs, debuffs, and control effects | Medium | Boss fights and damage-check stages |
| Starter | Cheap, accessible early attackers | Low — replace quickly | Your first few regions only |
| Specialized | One defining mechanic for a specific problem | Situational | Niche stages and enemy types |

A reliable beginner team usually looks like this: one or two damage units carrying the damage, one area unit to handle swarms, and one utility unit once you can afford the slot. Starter units hold those spots until something better arrives — and something better always arrives.

Community reports consistently point to the same trap: new players over-invest in the first unit they unlock because it feels strong in the tutorial regions. It isn't. Upgrade it enough to clear content, then move your resources to whatever replaces it.

## Farming Routes and Upgrade Priority

Not all game modes are worth your time at every stage of progression. Match the mode to your current bottleneck instead of grinding whatever is at the top of the menu.

| Mode | Primary Purpose | Reward Focus | Best Time to Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main story stages | Unlock new content and regions | Progression + first-clear rewards | Whenever you can clear them |
| Raids and challenges | Test specialized teams | Higher-value boss rewards | After your core team is stable |
| Special events | Limited-time exclusive rewards | Event currency and unique units | While the event is active |
| Farming runs | Repeatable resource income | Currency, materials, summon resources | When you hit a progression wall |

A simple priority list for spending what you earn:

- **First:** upgrades for the units that appear in every single run.
- **Second:** summons that fill a genuine gap in your roster.
- **Third:** traits on established core units.
- **Last:** duplicate investment and collection goals.

If you're ever unsure where to spend, ask which choice helps you clear the next stage. That question resolves most spending dilemmas on its own.

## Traits and Summoning: Where Rare Resources Go

Traits are rerollable modifiers attached to individual units. The rule is simple: keep traits that strengthen a unit's actual job, and reroll the ones that pull it in the wrong direction.

| Unit Role | Keep the Trait If… | Reroll If… |
|---|---|---|
| Damage dealer | It raises damage, attack speed, or range | It boosts a stat the unit never uses |
| Area coverage | It widens coverage or increases group damage | It only helps against single targets |
| Utility | It extends effect duration or reach | It adds personal damage while the unit's value is its buff |
| Economy | It accelerates early income in real runs | The unit has left your farming rotation |
| Starter | Almost never worth a reroll | You plan to replace the unit soon |

Summoning follows the same logic. Spend early summon resources on units that unlock and clear main stages, because immediate account value beats chasing duplicates. Shard- and soul-based resources are long-term currency — compare a targeted acquisition against a handful of random pulls before committing.

Limited banners change the math. If your current roster already clears the content you need, banking resources for a banner that fills a real gap is usually the stronger play. The community wiki tracks current banners and event rotations if you want to plan around them.

## Five Mistakes That Slow New Players Down

- **Ignoring codes.** Redeemable codes grant free resources, and the community wiki maintains an active list. Redemption is a quick menu entry in-game, so there's no reason to skip it.
- **Spreading upgrades evenly.** Concentration beats distribution in almost every tower defense, and this one is no exception.
- **Skipping stage objectives.** Objective rewards pay out resources you'll need later, and they're often easier than a full clear.
- **Rerolling traits too early.** Rare trait materials are scarce. Save them for units that survive your next roster change.
- **Chasing every new unit.** A built, upgraded unit you already own usually outperforms a shiny new one sitting at level one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Undertale Adventures X free to play?**
Yes. It's a free Roblox experience, so you can jump in without spending anything. Any spending beyond that is optional and cosmetic or convenience-based.

**How do I redeem codes in this game?**
Open the in-game codes menu, type the code exactly as published — capitalization and spacing matter — and confirm. Expired codes, extra spaces, and already-claimed codes are the usual reasons a redemption fails. This Undertale Adventures X beginner guide recommends checking a maintained code list rather than random videos, since fake code pages are common.

**Which units should a beginner prioritize?**
Start with affordable damage units, upgrade the one that performs best, and add an area-coverage unit for dense waves. Save rare materials for the units that stay in your main team across multiple stages.

**How often should I reroll traits?**
Only after your core team is set. Roll traits on the two or three units you use in every run, then stop. Rerolling starter units is one of the fastest ways to waste resources in the early game.

Get the loop right — coverage, concentrated upgrades, steady farming, then traits — and the Underground opens up far faster than grinding blindly ever will.

## How the Core Loop Works: Waves, Units, and Traits

Before you can survive a long run, you need to understand what the game is actually tracking. Undertale Adventures X keeps it simple on the surface: monsters walk toward your base, your units hit them, and you spend currency to get stronger. Under that surface, four systems do all the work.

| System | What It Does | Why It Matters in Long Runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wave spawning | Sends monsters along lanes toward your base | With no final wave, difficulty keeps climbing until you break |
| Unit placement | You position Undertale units to block and damage | Positioning compounds — a weak slot hurts more at wave 50 than wave 5 |
| Unit leveling | Currency raises a unit's raw stats | Keeps early units relevant deep into a run |
| Traits | Random bonuses applied to units | Multipliers, not additions — this is where carry units are made |
| Base health | Your loss condition | A single leak at high waves can end an entire run |

The important part is the relationship between leveling and traits. Leveling is linear and predictable; traits are the swing factor. A well-leveled unit with a mediocre trait will eventually fall behind a decent unit with a strong one. Community reports consistently point to trait quality as the difference between a 30-wave run and a 70-wave run.

If you're new to the source material the units are pulled from, the [official Undertale site](https://undertale.com/) is a good primer on the characters and tone the game borrows from. It won't teach you wave scaling, but it helps you understand why certain units feel like they *should* be strong.

A few practical habits for the early game:

- **Build wide before you build tall.** Two mid-tier units cover more lanes than one expensive unit.
- **Never skip economy.** Anything that generates currency pays for itself in a long run.
- **Bank before you gamble.** Rerolling traits with an empty wallet is how runs die at wave 15.
- **Watch the lane, not the health bar.** Your base health is a lagging indicator. Leaks start before you notice them.

## Unit Roles and Team Compositions for Long Runs

Most failed runs aren't lost to bad units — they're lost to bad compositions. Endless mode rewards a balanced roster far more than it rewards a pile of your favorite damage dealers.

| Role | Job | Priority in Endless Runs | Common Mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early DPS | Clears the first waves cheaply | High — you need a fast start | Over-investing here and never moving on |
| Crowd control | Slows or groups incoming monsters | Very high — scaling enemies need stalling | Treating it as optional |
| Single-target | Melts bosses and high-health elites | High — this is your run-ender insurance | Ignoring it until a boss shows up |
| Support | Buffs nearby units or debuffs enemies | Medium-high — multiplies everything else | Placing it out of range of your carries |
| Economy | Generates currency passively | Essential early, replaceable late | Selling it too early or keeping it too long |

A reliable template that shows up again and again in player experience: two to three damage units, one crowd-control unit, one support unit, and an economy unit that gets phased out once your carry is online. That's six slots doing five jobs. Anything more is luxury; anything less is a gap that high waves will find.

### Trait Priority, Ranked

- **Damage multipliers** — the backbone of every deep run. Stack these on your carry.
- **Attack speed** — pairs multiplicatively with damage; excellent on single-target units.
- **Range** — quietly powerful, since it lets one unit cover two lanes.
- **Utility traits** (slow, stun, armor shred) — situationally game-changing, especially on crowd control.
- **Flat stat boosts** — fine early, forgettable late.

## Endless Mode Strategy: How to Push Deeper

Long runs follow a recognizable arc. If you know which phase you're in, you know what to spend on.

| Run Phase | What Changes | Your Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waves 1–10 | Monsters are weak and slow | Economy, cheap coverage, no overbuilding |
| Waves 11–30 | Health scaling becomes noticeable | Upgrade carries, reroll traits, bank currency |
| Waves 31–60 | Leaks start happening in clusters | Tighten positioning, add support, cover gaps |
| Waves 61+ | Everything is a threat | Micro-manage, sell dead weight, rebuild around your best unit |

The single biggest behavioral difference between average and strong players is *when* they spend. Weak runs spend the moment they can afford something. Strong runs hold currency until a purchase meaningfully changes their damage output.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | The Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upgrading everything evenly | Spreads currency thin with no carry | Pick one or two units and commit |
| Skipping economy early | You fall behind the scaling curve permanently | Buy economy in the first ten waves |
| Chasing rare traits on weak units | Wastes currency on a unit you'll replace | Reroll only on units you plan to keep |
| Playing selfishly in co-op | Servers hold up to 15 players; lanes overlap | Coordinate roles before wave 20 |
| Ignoring base health until it's low | One leak ends the run | Track it every ten waves |

Co-op deserves special mention. With servers supporting up to 15 players, a coordinated group can cover far more ground than a solo run — but only if people divide roles instead of all building the same carry. Community reports suggest that disorganized lobbies wipe earlier than solo players who know exactly what they're doing.

## FAQ

**Does Undertale Adventures X actually have an endless mode?**
The game is built around wave-based tower defense, and the community's version of endless play is pushing survival runs as deep as possible. Because modes and wave structures change with updates, check the in-game menu for the current options rather than relying on older guides — the strategy for Undertale Adventures X endless mode stays the same either way.

**What's the best unit for deep runs?**
There isn't one. The best unit is whichever one you've committed levels and strong traits to, placed where it covers the most traffic. Community reports favor damage multipliers stacked on a single carry over spreading upgrades across a full roster.

**Should I play endless runs solo or with a team?**
Solo gives you full control and full rewards. Teams of up to 15 players can cover more lanes, but only with clear role division. If your lobby is chaotic, solo is often the faster path to a deep wave.

**How do I stop dying around wave 30?**
Almost always economy or positioning. Build income in the first ten waves, keep your support unit inside range of your carry, and hold currency until a purchase actually changes your damage output.

**Is endless mode worth the time investment?**
For players who enjoy optimization, yes — it's the clearest measure of whether your roster is actually good. For casual players, the AFK Chamber and shorter main-mode runs deliver similar progression with far less pressure.

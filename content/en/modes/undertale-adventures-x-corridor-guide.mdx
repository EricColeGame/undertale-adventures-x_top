Neutral Corridor Act 1 in Undertale Adventures X is a long, narrow lane, and that shape decides everything. Any Undertale Adventures X corridor guide worth its salt opens with the same warning: range beats raw damage on this map, because enemies march in a straight line toward your base and only your reach can stop them early. If your last attempt collapsed into a pile of leaks before the boss even appeared, the problem usually isn't your roster — it's your coverage.

This guide walks through the team composition community clears rely on, the leader buff that makes it function, and the economy decisions that separate a clean win from a wipe. None of it requires rare units. It requires you to stop placing towers the way you would on an open map.

## Recommended Team Composition for Neutral Corridor Act 1

Successful runs tend to converge on a five-part structure: a leader that stretches your range, a heavy damage dealer that inherits that stretch, a debuffer for the boss, a backline safety net, and enough economy units to pay for all of it. Player experience shared in walkthroughs points to the setup below.

| Unit | Primary Role | Why It Earns a Slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mettaton NEO | Leader / Buffer | Passive crit buff plus a leader skill that transforms qualifying CORE units |
| Knight Knight | Main DPS | Explosive-typed CORE unit that inherits every part of the leader buff |
| Woshua | Boss Debuffer | Applies soak, the debuff community runs treat as mandatory for Sans |
| Muffet | Backline Defense | Channeled bone attacks clean up anything that slips past the front line |
| Money Units | Economy | Fund placements and upgrades throughout the act |

Mettaton NEO anchors the team. Beyond its leader skill, it carries a passive critical hit buff that quietly raises your team's average damage for the whole run. Knight Knight is the payoff — it matches the leader skill's requirements, so it receives the full buff rather than a fraction, and it turns that extra reach into steady damage down the hall.

Woshua exists for one reason: soak. Muffet covers the failure case, catching fast or unusually tanky enemies that push through your choke point. Money units round out the roster — they don't kill anything themselves, but nothing else gets upgraded without them.

Worth saying plainly: exact wave counts, enemy health pools, and precise tile coordinates are not documented in official sources or available run footage. Treat this Undertale Adventures X corridor guide as a framework you adapt, not a script you follow tile by tile.

## How Mettaton NEO's Leader Buff Works

Mettaton NEO's leader skill is conditional, and that condition shapes your entire roster: it only enhances CORE category units that carry an explosive secondary typing. Build around that restriction and the corridor becomes manageable. Ignore it and you're paying for a buff that nobody on the field actually receives.

| Buff | Effect | What It Means in Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +50% Range | Extends attack reach | Units start hitting enemies far earlier in the lane |
| +25% Telegraph Size | Widens the affected area | More enemies caught per attack |
| Reduced SBA | Fewer seconds between attacks | Faster attack cycle and higher DPS uptime |
| Crit Buff | Passive critical hit chance | Higher average damage across the team |

The range increase is the headline. On a map shaped like a hallway, reach functions as a damage multiplier — a unit that begins firing 50% earlier gets several extra volleys off before enemies reach your choke point. The larger telegraph area compounds that by catching more of the wave per shot, while the reduced SBA shortens the gap between attacks so your damage never really pauses.

That is why Knight Knight pairs so naturally with Mettaton NEO. It satisfies both the CORE and explosive requirements, so it collects the entire buff instead of a sliver of it. When you're swapping units in and out, check those two tags before you check anything else — a stronger unit that misses the buff often underperforms a weaker one that gets it.

## Placement, Economy, and Leak Control

### Build Along the Center Track

Because the leader buff is range-based, placement matters more than raw stats. Community reports suggest running your main damage along the center of the corridor so the extended reach covers as much of the lane as possible. A unit hugging one wall wastes half its coverage on empty floor, and empty floor doesn't kill anything.

### Buy Economy Before You Buy Damage

Place your money units early. Every round you delay is currency you never earn back, and this act rewards sustained investment in your damage dealer far more than a wide spread of cheap units. A thin front line with strong upgrades tends to beat a crowded one with weak upgrades — a habit this Undertale Adventures X corridor guide keeps returning to.

### Upgrade in Priority Order

| Priority | Target | Why It Comes Here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Money units (early rounds) | Compounding currency funds everything else |
| 2 | Knight Knight damage | Your primary answer to every wave |
| 3 | Woshua reliability | Soak needs to land the moment Sans arrives |
| 4 | Muffet coverage | Insurance against leaks, not your main damage |
| 5 | Leader support | Small gains, but they lift the whole team |

### Fix These Mistakes First

| Mistake | What It Looks Like | The Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short-range towers | Enemies stroll past untouched | Field only CORE units with explosive typing under the buff |
| Late economy | You can't afford upgrades by mid-act | Place money units in the opening rounds |
| No backline | One fast enemy ends the run | Keep Muffet covering the rear |
| Skipping soak | Sans outlasts your damage | Bring Woshua and apply soak before committing |
| Ignoring tags | The leader buff applies to nobody | Verify CORE category and explosive typing |

## Taking Down Sans with Soak and Cleanup

Sans is the real gate on this stage, and player testing is consistent on one point: soaking him is not optional. Woshua's soak debuff is the mechanic that makes the boss fight winnable instead of a slow race you eventually lose.

| Boss Phase | Your Job | Unit Doing the Work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approach | Keep the lane clear so Sans arrives alone | Knight Knight |
| Engage | Apply soak so your damage actually sticks | Woshua |
| Sustain | Maintain DPS uptime with upgrades | Knight Knight |
| Cleanup | Catch anything that slips through | Muffet |

Clearing Judgement Hall / Neutral Corridor Act 1 is the method players use to unlock Sans, so this fight doubles as a reward check. If you're running the stage specifically for that unlock, resist the urge to over-invest in defense — the corridor rewards reach and sustained damage, not a wall of low-tier units.

This is also where a lot of runs stall out. Sans punishes slow damage, and slow damage is usually a symptom of misplaced units rather than a weak roster. Before you blame your team, walk your placement back and ask whether every unit is genuinely inside the leader buff's coverage. If it isn't, move it.
